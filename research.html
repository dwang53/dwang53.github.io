NASA Delta-X: Mississippi River Delta Evolution

HPC Geomorphology
2022 – Present

Numerical modeling of the Mississippi River Delta's land evolution and climate-risk assessment, enabling deltas to thrive in a century of rising seas. This project develops high-resolution morphodynamic models to simulate sediment transport and land accretion processes across the Wax Lake and Atchafalaya deltas, informing coastal-resilience strategies under accelerating sea-level rise.

Freshwater Flocculation & Deltaic Wetland Sustainability

HPC Geomorphology
2022 – Present

Investigating the impact of clay flocculation on sustaining floodplains and deltaic wetlands. Direct numerical simulation (DNS) and large-eddy simulation (LES) model the micro-scale physics of floc formation and break-up; these processes are then upscaled into macro-scale sediment-transport models for river deltas. Results have direct implications for coastal land-loss mitigation in Louisiana and deltaic regions worldwide.

Extreme Weather Mudflow & Debris Flow Simulation

HPC
2021 – 2022

Simulation of mudflow and debris flow during a 1,000-year precipitation event in Palmdale, CA, focusing on climate-related extreme weather and safety / risk analysis. High-fidelity computational fluid dynamics models predicted flow pathways, inundation extent, and impact forces on infrastructure, providing critical data for emergency management and urban planning.

Great Lakes Watershed & Climate Change Impacts on CAWS

HPC Remote Sensing
2018 – 2022

Investigation of the impact of climate change and invasive-species mitigation on the Chicago Area Waterway System (CAWS), relating to flooding, water quality, and navigation. High-resolution 3-D numerical modeling combined with remote sensing quantified how Lake Michigan water-level rise drives complex bidirectional flows and invasive-species migration pathways. The research informed a potential 10% increase in allowable water use by the State of Illinois.

Density-Driven Mixing Layers in Environmental Flows

HPC
2015 – 2022

Investigation of mixing layers and surface patterns in the Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal driven by horizontal density gradients. DNS and LES via NEK5000 revealed the instability mechanisms governing the spatiotemporal evolution of these interfacial layers, with implications for pollutant transport and water quality in engineered channels. This work formed the core of the Ph.D. dissertation.

Large Wood Debris (LWD) Impact on Meandering Rivers

HPC Geomorphology
2013 – 2014

Quantifying the effects of large wood debris (LWD) patches on flow resistance and bank erosion in evolving meandering rivers. Using OpenTelemac coupled with a bank-erosion module, this project produced the first systematic numerical analysis of how LWD accumulation affects secondary circulation, sediment routing, and long-term channel migration rates, providing guidance for river restoration and habitat management.
